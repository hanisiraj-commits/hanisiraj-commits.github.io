08

List growth had stalled, and it is the one input the whole lifecycle depends on.

Across Q2 the store more than doubled its monthly revenue while sign-ups moved 1.9%. Rippl sells one machine once, so there is no reorder to catch and no subscription to renew. Every dollar lifecycle earns next quarter has to come from someone who joined the list this quarter. Alia is what changed that.

Alia · why this is the number that matters

A one-time purchase business runs out of people, not out of ideas.

On a subscription or a consumable, lifecycle can grow by selling the same customer again. Rippl cannot. The welcome flow converts a new subscriber once and then that person is done, which makes top of funnel the ceiling on everything downstream.

The consequence

A list growing at 1.9% a quarter behind a store growing at 141% means the lifecycle channel was being asked to do more with the same people. That works for one quarter and then it stops. Continuous top of funnel is not a nice-to-have on this account, it is the only way the channel grows at all.

Three tests, one answer

  • Money off the machine won every time. It beat a 50% discount on a replacement filter, it beat Alia's own SmartTrigger automation, and it beat a mystery-discount tease. Three different challengers, same result.
  • The audience wants the number on the screen. "$100 Off Rippl Pure" beat "You've Got A Mystery Discount" on the same traffic, same offer underneath.
  • The pop-up going into the launch has no number on it. The live Nano pop-up is waitlist capture with no offer, which may be right before a launch, but it is the one thing every test so far argues against. Worth deciding on purpose rather than by default.

The delay test splits traffic almost perfectly, 198 against 190 against 194, so whatever the sign-up rates say the result is trustworthy. It is a decision the room can take today. Note that Alia's internal variant names have drifted from the creative: the winner of test 03 is filed as "$50 off Rippl pure" while the pop-up on screen reads $100 off, so the screenshots are the reliable record.
