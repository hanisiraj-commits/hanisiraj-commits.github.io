01

Lifecycle stopped selling in Q3 and started building the list.

It drove 34% of quarterly revenue off five inherited flows, then spent August turning paid traffic into the audience the Nano launch will be sold to. Sign-ups grew every month while revenue fell.

Customer lifecycle · the finding

The same offer was worth 38 times more to a small list than a big one.

The EOFY early access send on 14 June went to 280 engaged profiles and returned $20.89 a recipient. The same offer, sent broad to 3,299, returned $0.55. Nothing changed but the size of the audience and the fact that it felt exclusive.

Worth claiming in the room

  • 87% of collected sign-ups reach Klaviyo. On another account we audited the same way, only 39% did. This integration is healthy and that is not the default.
  • Sign-up volume rose 39% from June to August while revenue fell 81%. Those two lines moving in opposite directions is the quarter working as planned.

Where the opportunity is

  • The 34% was earned without a retention layer at all. Everything above the line is acquisition and recovery. Nothing in the account has ever been asked to make a second sale.
  • 94.8% first-time buyers is a product fact before it is a lifecycle one. A $949 appliance on a 12 to 24 month filter cycle has nothing to reorder. Nano's 2-month filter is the first repeatable thing this business has sold.

The two jobs this structure does

  • It protects the inbox. Opening on the smallest, warmest audience banks a strong engagement signal before volume arrives, which is what carries the later waves into the inbox.
  • It reproduces the $20.89 send instead of the $0.55 one. Every wave is small enough to feel addressed rather than broadcast.

Two glass bottles, a Water Ritual Guide, and a free replacement filter delivered at month 2. The real question is not whether to do it but which flow it belongs to: as a welcome incentive it competes with the discount above, as a post-purchase gift it lifts retention instead of acquisition, and as a subscription hook it prices the annuity. Those are three different builds.

03

Q4 is where lifecycle stops being an acquisition channel.

The filter is the first repeatable purchase this business has sold. Between that, a real review base and a list built before November rather than during it, Q4 is the first quarter lifecycle can be asked for retention revenue.

Q4 in planning · the annuity

The replenishment mechanic, spelled out.

Named in the deck as a 2-month cycle. Here is what actually has to be built, because the branch logic is what decides whether it earns anything.

ElementHow it works
TriggerPlaced order containing a Nano machine, with the clock starting at delivery rather than purchase.
CycleFirst prompt at 7 weeks, ahead of the 2-month filter life, so the replacement lands before the on-screen indicator starts flashing at 10%.
BranchSubscribers get a shipment notice and an upsell. One-time buyers get the reorder plus the case for switching to subscription, which is where the conversion sits.
Second leverFree shipping threshold set $1 under three filter sets, so the reorder self-selects into a bigger basket.
Sizing500 launch units against filter attach rate. That number lands in the first three weeks of October and it sizes the whole retention line.

Two things the Q4 plan does not currently account for.
