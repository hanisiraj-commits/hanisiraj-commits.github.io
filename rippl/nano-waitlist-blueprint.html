Blueprint · Pass 1 · 14/08/2026 · F_NANO-WAITLIST-NURTURE · 3 emails · structure only, no copy yet

Why this flow has to exist, and why it carries more than a thank-you.

The campaign sequence in drafts v3 and v4 excludes the Rippl Nano Early Access list from campaigns 1, 2 and 3, and only picks them up again on campaign 4, the reveal. So from the moment someone joins the waitlist, they are dark. They raised their hand, and then the only brand that has their attention says nothing to them for weeks while the cold list gets the tease, the PFAS story and the lab results.

That means this flow is not a confirmation email with two follow-ups. It is the entire pre-launch experience for your highest-intent audience, and it has to carry the same argument the campaigns carry, told better, because these people already said yes once.

Three emails, Day 0, Day 3, Day 8. Confirm and anchor, then prove, then make it theirs.

Five things found while pulling this together, two of which change existing work

1. The mineral claim is unblocked. Drafts v3 and v4 both stall on the same thing: you wanted Nano to name the minerals it retains, and I held it at "doesn't strip your water bare" because the ALS report had no mineral breakdown. The live waitlist page now states it publicly: "Calcium and magnesium independently confirmed present in filtered water." That is already published on your own site, so it is approved language. The blocked slot in campaign emails 3 and 4 can be written now.

2. The filter price in the playbook is wrong. Vault has $45 every 2 to 3 months. The live page says $59.99. The page wins, and the $0.80 a day maths on the page is built off it. Worth correcting in the vault so it stops propagating.

3. It is SGS and ALS. The playbook only knows about ALS. The page credits both, and leads with SGS. There is also a UV sterilisation stage that appears nowhere in the vault. Two-stage: UF nano filtration, then UV.

4. The 57 people already on the list have had nothing. Early access ads went live 11/08 and the list is at 57 as of today. When this flow switches on it only catches new joiners. Backfill decision needed, options in the table at the bottom.

5. I could not watch the founder video. You said it is on the success state after the form submits. The waitlist page renders that inline and I could not reach it without putting a real email onto the client's list, which I was not going to do. Send me the file or the URL and E1 gets built around it properly.

Email 2
What's actually in your water
Problem aware to solution aware · the belief-builder
Day 3+3 days
The job
This is the most important email in the sequence and the reason the flow exists. It carries the whole argument the waitlist is suppressed from receiving as campaigns: the problem nothing else filters, and the independent proof that Nano does. Someone who reads this email properly is no longer deciding whether to buy a water filter, only which one.
Content spine
  • What PFAS actually is. Most people have heard the term and could not define it. Non-stick pans, food packaging, firefighting foam, waterproof jackets, made since the 1950s.
  • Why forever. They do not break down. Decades in the soil and water, no natural process clears them.
  • The gap. Treatment plants kill bacteria and clear sediment and do it well. PFAS was not on the list when those plants were designed. Only granular activated carbon and ion-exchange resin remove it.
  • The rules moving. Australia cut the safe limit roughly 90%. PFOS came down from 70 ng/L to 8. That is the one hard number in the email and it is doing the work.
  • The turn, into proof. Tested across 38 PFAS compounds, none found. No lead, arsenic or mercury. Zero bacteria. UV stage after filtration.
  • The part that is better than clean. Calcium and magnesium independently confirmed present. Removing everything is easy. Removing only what should not be there is the hard version, and it is now a claim we can actually make.
  • Credibility line. Independently tested by SGS and ALS, NATA accredited.

Email 3
One machine, and which finish is yours
Product aware · pre-decide the purchase
Day 8+5 days
The job
Move them from "this is legitimate" to "this is mine". Everything here is designed so that on launch day the decision is already made and the only remaining action is clicking. The strongest lever is not the filtration story, it is that Nano quietly deletes three things they already own.
Content spine
  • The running cost. $0.80 a day. Bottled water runs the average Australian household over $120 a month. One filter every 2 to 3 months at $59.99 is the only ongoing cost.
  • The kettle is redundant. 100°C on demand, 6°C cold, five presets between, three seconds. This is the line that lands hardest and it is currently buried halfway down the landing page.
  • Nothing to install. Out of the box, on the bench, plug in, fill the 3.5L tank. No plumber, no drilling, no permanent change to the kitchen.
  • Nothing to manage. No wastewater tank, nothing to drain. On-screen indicator flashes at 10% so you are never guessing about the filter.
  • What it replaces. Jug filter, kettle, bottled water delivery, all one machine. This is the comparison logic from the page, compressed.
  • Black or white. Its own section, both finishes shown at equal weight, same angle and light. Visual only per your call, single CTA underneath.
  • Close on readiness. Launching September, waitlist hears first, be ready to move. See open decision 2 on how hard to lean on the stock split.

Open decisions

DecisionMy recommendationWhy it matters
1. How precise on the launch date Say September, hold the exact window The page says "Launching September 2026". The vault has a Sep 20 to 26 window agreed 04/08. If we put a date in email and it slips, the waitlist is the audience that notices and the trust cost is highest. Vague and kept beats specific and missed. If the window is locked hard, tell me and E1 and E3 both tighten.
2. Do we use the 250/250 stock split as scarcity Yes in E3, softly, no numbers The split is real and it genuinely constrains colour choice, so it is honest scarcity. But publishing "250 units" invites a run and it sits against the standing rule on never exposing sales quantities. "Stock is split between the two finishes and we cannot move units from one to the other" gets the whole effect without a number.
3. Do we mention price at all No. Hold it for launch day $599 subscribe and save versus $675 one-time was locked 04/08 but is nowhere on the public page. Price is the single biggest lever you have on launch day and revealing it early spends it for nothing. E3 uses the $0.80 a day running cost instead, which is the friendlier number anyway.
4. The 57 already on the list Backfill them through the flow They joined off the 11/08 ads and have heard nothing. Cleanest fix is to switch the flow on, then add the existing 57 with backfill enabled so they run the same three emails. Alternative is a one-off catch-up campaign, which is more work for a worse result. Your call, but leaving them dark is the one option I would rule out.
5. Founder video Send me the file or URL Only hard blocker on drafting. E1 can be written around it as a marked slot, but if the video says something specific about what waitlisters get, the copy should lead into it rather than talk over it.
