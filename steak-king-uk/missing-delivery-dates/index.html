Why orders arrive without a delivery date

Orders keep landing with no delivery date attached, and nobody could reproduce it by hand. We traced every recent case to its source. This is what we found, how we found it, and the fix that closes it for good.

01The short version

One specific browser is breaking the date picker

Not random, not customer error, and not the Taster Box page.

When a customer taps an ad or link in Instagram, the page opens inside Instagram's own built-in browser rather than Safari or Chrome. In roughly 1 in 3.5 of those sessions, the delivery date picker on the cart page silently fails to load. The customer sees a normal-looking cart, is never asked for a date, checks out successfully, and the order lands dateless. Nothing looks wrong to them at any point.

02How we traced it

400 orders, three eliminations

We split the last 400 orders into dated and dateless, then followed the differences.

  • The picker never touched the dateless orders. Every order with a date carries the date picker's fingerprint on its line items. All 21 dateless orders carry none — the date was never captured, not lost later.
  • All 21 went through the cart page. Each has a cart record, ruling out Buy It Now buttons or any shortcut that skips the cart.
  • The browser on each order settled it. Shopify records the customer's browser at checkout:

04The mechanism

The system fails open

The date requirement only exists as a script running in the customer's browser. If that script dies, nothing else enforces the date.

The checkout button underneath the picker is a standard Shopify button that works on its own; the picker's script is what disables it until a date is chosen. Inside Instagram's browser that script intermittently dies before it can draw the picker or lock the button. Two known behaviours of in-app browsers explain it: they restrict access to browser storage, which the picker's code reads without any fallback, and they load scripts slowly enough that the cart can look complete — with a live checkout button — before the picker arrives.

We confirmed the failure path end to end in a controlled test: with the picker's script out of the picture, the cart looks clean and Shopify accepts the order with no date.

05Why it went unnoticed

You can't catch this by testing on a phone

The picker works in 71% of Instagram sessions, and whether it fails depends on the state of the customer's device: the Instagram browser's stored data, privacy settings, connection speed. A team member testing on their own phone will almost always land in the working 71%, refresh after refresh — we tried, and so did you. That is exactly why this went unnoticed while the orders quietly accumulated. It took the order data, not a phone in hand, to see the pattern.

06The HK comparison

Why HK doesn't have this problem

The same analysis on Steak King HK found zero missing dates across recent web orders. HK's traffic is mostly Google-led, so far fewer customers arrive inside Instagram's browser, and the HK cart forces an extra mandatory agreement step before checkout. The UK exposure comes from Meta-led acquisition — which is doing its job — colliding with a date picker that can't survive Instagram's browser.

07The fix

Enforce the date where the browser can't break it

Client-side patches can't be trusted against a browser we don't control. The permanent fix is server-side.

Steak King UK is on Shopify Plus, which supports checkout validation rules: a rule that refuses to complete any checkout with no delivery date attached (with an exemption for Express Delivery items). It works identically in every browser, in-app or not, and closes the gap permanently. Available through the date picker app's checkout validation option on Plus, or as a small custom build.

Already in place as a safety net: an automatic internal alert inside the store. The moment any order lands without a date, the team receives an email with the customer's name, phone and email plus a direct link to the order, so the date can be confirmed before fulfilment instead of discovered later.
