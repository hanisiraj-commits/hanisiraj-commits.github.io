What the UK list told us

We ran two surveys across the list at the start of September. One asked 51 buyers how their order went. One asked 347 people who have never ordered what was stopping them. 385 finished. Everything they told us is below, along with what we would do about it.

01Our read

Two things came out of this. Everything else is detail.

We wrote the two surveys separately and asked different questions. They arrived at the same two conclusions from opposite ends of the funnel, which is the main reason we would act on them.

The first thing

The range is priced for a customer who is not on this list

The position today

  • The cheapest four-steak pack is the M4-5 Sirloin 1kg at £84.
  • The M9+ range runs £114 to £124.
  • Nothing else on the site sits under £84 except a 2kg brisket at £56.
  • The standing offer is £20 off £75.

What the list says

  • 78% expect four premium wagyu steaks to cost under £75. Only 4% expect over £100.
  • Buyers score value for money 58%, the only score under 80%.
  • Buyers' number one request is more affordable everyday cuts.
  • Non-buyers' number two lever is more affordable steaks.

What we recommend

Add a step below the range. Don't discount the top of it.

An entry product at £45 to £60. Two steaks rather than four. That lands inside the band where 78% of the list already sits, and it turns the £84 pack into a trade-up instead of the front door.

The price is not wrong for the product, and we would not touch it. Buyers who have tried it say so: 75% rate Steak King fair or better value against other wagyu. The problem is that there is no way in under £84.

The second thing

This list buys meat by looking at it. We are asking them to buy on trust.

The position today

  • Product pages lead on grade and weight in kilos.
  • Portion counts and pack contents are not consistently shown.
  • Reviews are not surfaced in email, and thinly on site.
  • Pack weights carry a stated tolerance of around 10%.

What the list says

  • 75% currently buy steak at a butcher, supermarket or farm shop. Only 17% buy meat online at all.
  • 79 non-buyers say they cannot tell how much meat they actually get.
  • 94 people (27%) doubt the quality or the brand outright.
  • 4 of 36 written buyer comments dispute the weight they received.

What we recommend

Merchandise the thing they can't hold.

Portion count and per-steak weight on every product page, a scale reference in the photography, a plain-English grade explainer, and reviews pushed into email and onto the PDP.

Then tighten the weight tolerance or state it in the open. A customer paying £124 a kilo will weigh it, and one bad weigh-in costs more than the margin on that order.

This costs no margin, which is why we would start here while the range work is being scoped.

04Recommendations

What we are recommending

Grouped by how much agreement each one needs before it can move. The first group we would start on now. The second needs a conversation with you. The third we would like to go away and dig into.

Where we would start

Our recommendation
  • Build an entry product at £45 to £60Two steaks, not four. It is the top request in both surveys and there is nothing on the site that answers it. Until it exists, 78% of the list has no way in.
  • Put portion count and per-steak weight on every product pagePlus a scale reference in the photography. This answers the 79 who cannot tell how much meat they get, and the four buyers who felt short-changed. It costs no margin, and we can brief it this week.
  • Resolve the weight toleranceEither tighten it or state it plainly at the point of sale. One customer put their shortfall at "nearly £40". At £124 a kilo people weigh it, and a disputed weigh-in undoes the 90% score the meat just earned.
  • Chase the 374 unredeemed £50 vouchers before they lapseThey expire 30 days from issue and 97% are still outstanding. It is the cheapest revenue on the table this month and we can have the reminder out in days.
  • Fix the Northern Ireland problemA subscriber signed up from an advert and then found the store does not deliver there. Either serve it or exclude it at acquisition. As it stands the spend is going on people who cannot be fulfilled.

To decide together

Needs your call
  • Does the entry tier come from a new SKU or a smaller cut of an existing one?A 500g two-steak pack cut from the M4-5 Sirloin gets there fastest. A purpose-bought entry line protects the flagship better. This is a margin and operations call rather than a marketing one, so it needs to come from your side.Owner: Dave
  • Do we hold the flagship price and add below it, or reprice the range?We would hold. Buyers say the rate is fair for the grade, and cutting it spends margin on a problem an entry product solves for nothing.Recommendation: hold
  • How loudly can we lead on halal?Two people wrote in that they could not confirm the meat was halal, one asking specifically for slaughterhouse codes. The certification is already held. We need to know what can be stated, and where.Owner: Dave
  • Free delivery as a lever123 non-buyers named it and 48 said delivery cost put them off. It is already free over £99, which points to a communication gap rather than a pricing one. Worth deciding together whether the threshold moves or whether we just say it louder.

What we would explore next

With your go-ahead
  • Is acquisition bringing in people who were never going to buy?Nine free-text answers cite unemployment, a pension or hardship, and several more say some version of "I'm skint at the moment". If the pop-up and the paid sources are pulling that bracket, every conversion rate in the reporting is being dragged down by an audience that was never going to clear £84. We would like to look at refining the targeting to exclude those profiles.
  • Reviews: what can we harvest, and can we borrow Hong Kong's?38 non-buyers said reviews would convert them and two said ours are poor or missing. Two jobs here. We would go and find what already exists online to pull onto the site, and in the meantime run the Hong Kong reviews on the UK store. Same product, same supply, and there are years of them sitting there already.
  • Expedite the range outside beefBuyers asked for burgers, sausages and mince (17), sharing joints and roasts (16), seafood (12) and lamb (7). Demand is already established, so the question we would take away is how fast the range can move rather than whether it should.
05The £50 voucher

One last thing, because it already worked

We paid for the responses with a £50 voucher against orders over £150. It was built to buy answers rather than to sell meat, so what happened next was a bonus.

3 September did 23 orders and £2,735 in sales, 2.7 times the daily order average and 2.8 times the daily sales average for the fortnight before. Ten of those 23 orders carried a survey voucher. A research send moved a trading day, which is not what we sent it to do.

What set the basket was the £150

Eleven baskets, and every one landed between £158 and £207. Nobody got close to £150 and stopped. Nobody blew past it either. People built a basket to clear the number and checked out, so the threshold set the order value rather than the discount. It lifted the average basket 37% above the store's normal £132.

Two operational notes. Ten of the eleven redemptions landed on day one, so a voucher like this gets spent immediately or not at all. The reminder window is days, not weeks. And 374 vouchers are still unredeemed, expiring 30 days from issue.

This mechanic existed to pay for a survey and we are not proposing it as a store offer. But the lesson is worth keeping: when we next build a threshold offer, the minimum is the lever we should be arguing about, not the discount.
