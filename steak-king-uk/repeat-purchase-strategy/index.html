Steak King UK · Lifecycle Strategy

Building the repeat-purchase funnel, benchmarked on Hong Kong

What 12 months of Hong Kong order data tells us about how a second purchase happens, and the funnel we want to set up in the UK to guide every first-time buyer towards it. HK is the compass here, not the map: different region, different audience — everything below is a starting hypothesis to test, not a playbook to copy.

Prepared 17/07/2026 · Reworked 21/07/2026 around the HK second-order journey analysis · Data pulled live from Klaviyo (UK + HK) and Shopify · Tethrd × Steak King

What the HK data points at

In Hong Kong the second order is a cross-sell, not a top-up: 77.5% of second orders contain nothing from the first, the reorder wave lands around days 22–39, and the customers most likely to come back are the ones buying everyday staples — chicken, mince, salmon — not the deal boxes. Direction for UK: merchandise a staples destination worth coming back to, give every order a built-in reason to return, and time our guidance to that window. Then test each of those assumptions against how UK customers actually behave.

The journey runs in two directions, and both matter.

  • Premium first, staples second. Wagyu-first buyers most often come back for more wagyu — but the biggest cross-category flows are wagyu → Black Angus, wagyu → fish and wagyu → poultry. The treat purchase comes first; the "fill the freezer for the week" basket comes second.
  • Staples first, wagyu second. Mince → wagyu and fish → wagyu are both top-ten transitions. Cheap everyday proteins are a genuine on-ramp to the premium range, not a dead end.

The flag for UK. Our acquisition hero — the £99 Wagyu Starter box with free knife — is structurally the same offer as HK's knife-bundle boxes, HK's worst-repeating meat gateway at ~14% (against a 27.8% average). Deal-box buyers skew deal-hunter. That doesn't mean changing the acquisition offer — it means the journey after the box has to work hardest, and the HK data suggests what it should sell: the everyday staples both halves of the journey converge on.

03The funnel we want to set up

Three pieces, each carrying a testable hypothesis

Each piece carries a hypothesis from the HK data that UK cohorts will confirm or kill. Together they give a first-time buyer a place to go back to, a reason to go back, and guidance on when and what.

Piece 1 · Merchandising the second order

A staples destination worth coming back to

  • A dedicated staples section on site. The UK store is built around the box; HK's second orders are built around everyday proteins. Stand up a restock collection (salmon fillets, chicken breast and thighs, mince, sausages) so a returning customer can pick up the value cut they actually want and fill the cart to the £99 free-shipping threshold in the same visit. Today that basket has nowhere to land.
  • Staple-anchored bundles. Build bundles with staples in them — a "Freezer Fill" pack priced at the £99 line, and staple add-ins on the premium boxes — alongside the BBQ pack and higher-ticket tiers already on the growth queue. HK hypothesis: staples in the basket predict ~1.5× repeat.
  • Seed the staple at order one. Test a low-price staple add-on at checkout (1kg mince at a token price, or free above a threshold). If the HK gateway pattern is causal, putting a staple in the first UK basket should lift repeat measurably — a cheap, clean experiment.
  • Keep the repeat products in stock. Back-in-stock notifications and stock priority for the staples that drive reorders — a repeat funnel pointing at out-of-stock products is worse than none.
Piece 2 · The reorder mechanic

Steak King Credit — every order earns the next one

  • The mechanic: every delivered order earns a fixed, visible store credit (proposal: 10% of order value, minimum £10), issued after delivery and expiring ~35 days later. The credit is the reason to come back; the expiry is the reason to come back now — timed to the day 22–39 window where HK's best repeaters naturally reorder.
  • It rides existing rails. The No Fee Day mechanic already issues coupon-as-store-credit via a Klaviyo flow with 30-day validity. This extends a proven pattern into an always-on economy — no new infrastructure.
  • It protects the price architecture. Credit is a visible £ figure with the customer's name on it (David's rule: offers customers can see), and it replaces blanket %-off on second orders — £79 on the box stays the floor we never cross.
  • Order-2 staple GWP as the bridge. A free staple (1kg mince or chicken, picked on margin) on the second order: visible, cheap, mirrors the referral-ladder mental model customers already know, and stocks the freezer with the habit product that drives order three.
  • Test it, don't assume it: pilot credit vs a control cohort with no mechanic; measure 60-day repeat rate and margin per customer, not just redemption.
Piece 3 · The guidance layer

Lifecycle touches that walk people down the journey

  • Two cross-sell paths, split on first-order contents. Box and premium buyers get the "stock your freezer" staples story; staple-first buyers get the wagyu step-up story. Both paths exist in the HK data — we guide, the first basket decides which script.
  • Replenish only what replenishes. Reorder nudges on staples (roughly monthly cadence in HK); premium boxes get storytelling and cross-sell, never "running low?" nags.
  • The credit loop is itself a sequence: credit issued on delivery → reminder → last-72-hours expiry push. Three touches pointing at the same reorder without a single sitewide discount.
  • Paid social as amplifier, not engine. Sync Klaviyo segments to Meta and retarget only 1× buyers inside their day 20–45 window with the same staples/step-up creative and a credit-expiry reminder, suppressing anyone who reorders. The audience is a few hundred people at a time, so spend stays small by construction — it simply makes the ad say what the email says. Jay builds from the same creatives.
  • Winback at day 45+ with a dedicated returning-customer offer — and the WELCOME20 leak closed so the acquisition code stops doing retention's job.

All discount depths, GWP picks, bundle SKUs and the credit % go through David's margin floors. No beer mechanics until the liquor licence lands.

04The repeat-purchaser journey

How it lands on one customer's calendar

The default path from first order to second, with the HK evidence for each beat. Days are from delivery, not purchase. This is the workflow we design and test against; branch timings will be tuned to UK behaviour as cohorts mature.

Day 0 · Order placed
Confirmation sets up the relationship

Welcome from Johnny, what happens next, and the first tease of Steak King Credit: "your first £X back is coming with your delivery." The next order is on the table before the first one arrives.

Day 2–3 · Delivery
Credit issued + cook-it-tonight guide

The credit email lands with the box: a visible £ balance, expiry date, and Johnny's method for the exact cuts they bought. The best retention email is the one that makes the first purchase taste great.

Day 5–7 · Post-purchase nurture
The eating moment — proof and pride

Education and provenance (marbling, halal certification, direct-from-source), review and UGC ask while the experience is fresh, referral ladder reminder.

HK: eager early repeaters (~7% by day 14) self-serve here — no discounting needed for them.
Day 10–14 · Listen, then point
Survey + soft "what to try next"

The post-delivery survey (already being built) plus the first cross-sell nudge, routed by first basket: box buyers see the staples range, staple buyers see the wagyu step-up.

Day 20–25 · The main cross-sell
"Stock your freezer" — staples story + credit reminder

The weeknight basket pitch: salmon, chicken, mince from the staples section, cart-filled to the £99 free-shipping line, credit balance front and centre. Meta retargeting layer switches on for this window with the same message.

HK: best-repeating products reorder at median 22–35 days; the second order is a cross-sell 77.5% of the time.
Day 30–35 · Credit expiry
Last-72-hours push

Honest urgency on the expiring balance — the "come back now" beat, no sitewide discount required.

HK: median gap to the second order is 39 days — expiry sits just ahead of the natural decision point.
Day 35–45 · Didn't bite?
The staple GWP offer

Free 1kg staple on their next order — a visible perk that also seeds the habit product for order three. Replenish nudges start here for staple-first buyers.

Day 45+ · Winback
Returning-customer offer, then patience

A dedicated returning-customer code (never WELCOME20), then the customer rolls into campaign land — LMTs, drops and seasonal moments do the long-tail reactivation.

HK: repeat rate keeps climbing from 23.5% at day 60 to 29.1% at day 90 — the tail is long, don't burn it with discount spam.
05What it's worth

Every point of repeat rate compounds

At the current ~330 new customers/month and £130 AOV, every point of repeat rate is real, compounding revenue. Second orders carry no basket decay, so this is close to pure incremental margin.

The targets aren't arbitrary — they're HK's own early-life numbers: 15% is HK's 30-day rate, 25% is its 60-day rate. Matching HK's new-customer behaviour gets us there; moving from today's 7.5% to 25% is worth roughly +£90k/year in incremental second-order revenue before any acquisition growth.
