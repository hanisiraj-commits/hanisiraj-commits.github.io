01 · REPEAT PURCHASE MODELLING

“Show the repeat purchase pattern for years two, three and four and the arithmetic behind it. Our finding from Shopify: the best chance of a repeat is the first three to four months, after which it falls off a cliff for about 18 months. Show what that window is worth and what the prize is.”

Years two, three and four add 0.15, 0.12 and ~0.11 orders per customer. Your four-month finding is right; the cliff is a slope that never recovers.

The pattern

Of 100 first-time online customers, 10 buy again within 4 months, 18 within 12, 25 within 24 and 29 within 36. The 71 who have not come back online in three years are the opportunity. For those who do return, the median gap to the second order is about six months.

Year 1 is the 12 months after each customer’s first order; inside the plan’s calendar year the same intake delivers about half of the Year-1 line, because the average customer has six months of exposure.

Your three-to-four-month finding, tested

Correct on the concentration: 45% of all second orders happen inside four months and 70% inside twelve. The first 30 days carry a 6% chance; months 1–4 about 2% a month; month 12 is 1.1%; months 18–23 are 0.6–0.7%. It is a steady decline, not a cliff, and there is no bump at 12, 18 or 24 months: the bra cycle does not appear in online behaviour because nothing prompts it. The model therefore does not bank an “18-month natural return”; it has to be triggered.

What the window is worth, and the prize

A customer who reorders inside four months goes on to place 0.32–0.37 orders in year two; a customer with no second order in 24 months places none, and the cohort average is 0.15. Once the window closes the monthly chance is 1–2% and falling. The prize is moving the repeat rate, and the second order inside the window, to these levels:

Our position, stated so it holds. The cycle we are building to is 12 months, with the second purchase inside the first four. Among customers who do reorder, the median gap is already about six months, so the cycle is not the stretch; getting the other 80% to a second order is. Two purchases in the first year for the average customer is a Nala-class outcome and a two-year target. How it is done: post-purchase and cross-sell in the first 30 days (where the chance is already 6%); a fit follow-up at weeks 4–6; replenishment triggers at months 6, 9 and 12 by the exact style and size bought; loyalty credit earned at any till, spent online, that expires; and store purchases entering the same journey. Category reference: makers and fitters advise replacing a bra every 6–12 months of regular wear; surveys put actual replacement at 18–24 months; your own Loyalty Life Cycle file has the FY23 in-store cohort at one transaction every 16–20 months. Behaviour runs at two to three times the product’s life.

02 · AOV

“Show the workings on AOV. We cannot see how the AOV increase is derived.”

  • The CPA. There is no $55. Reaching the pool is sending cost plus whatever offer or credit is used: $10–20 a customer on 6,300, not $55. If paid retargeting of the file is planned it is budgeted in ad spend at a remarketing CPA and still counted as returning.
