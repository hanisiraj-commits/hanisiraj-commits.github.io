01The call

Two clocks, not one channel decision

Because cohorts repeat every two to three months, this is not a 25-day campaign. It is a 25-day sprint bolted onto an engine build, and the two need different channels.

Recommendation

Fill Cohort 01 with warm audiences and precise targeting. Build the always-on engine underneath it, and let Meta earn its place from Cohort 02 onward.

The instinct to skip Meta creative testing is right, but the reason matters. Meta is not the wrong channel. It is the wrong channel for the 31 August deadline. Over a full year of cohorts it is likely the volume engine, because the learning you buy compounds across every cohort that follows.

Clock 1 — the 25 days

Meta needs roughly 50 conversions per ad set per week to exit learning. Cohort 01 offers ten, total. Add a 2 to 3 week creative testing cycle and the window shuts before there is anything worth scaling. For this clock: warm, precise, manual.

Clock 2 — the next twelve months

Four to six cohorts a year at ten-plus seats is ₹60L to ₹90L of annual revenue and, more usefully, 200 to 400 applications a year. That is enough conversion signal for Meta to optimise properly, so long as you optimise on the application, not the seat.

02What the India search data says

The category has no search demand. The underlying job has plenty.

Two findings from live India-geo query data, and they pull in opposite directions.

Finding one — nobody searches for the product

Query expansion on education fellowship india returns teacher fellowships, teaching fellowships and scholarships. Google's model does not recognise "a fellowship for people who want to build a school" as a concept that exists. There is no bottom-funnel category demand to capture. This is a category-creation product, and search is structurally poor at those.

Finding two — the job to be done is densely searched

how to start a school in india carries a deep state-level tail: Karnataka, Bangalore, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Telangana, Delhi, Jharkhand. That depth is a reliable signal of sustained, real volume. Alongside it sit cost to start a school in india, requirements to start a school and microschools in india.

The catch is intent. That searcher wants a licence, a compliance checklist, a franchise or a consultant. BoE sells a nine-week cohort on rethinking what a school is. Closing that gap on cold search is paid for in conversion rate, which is exactly where search's quality advantage gets spent.

04The math

Search caps out at two seats a cohort

The funnel requirement and the available inventory are an order of magnitude apart, and that gap does not close with more budget.

What ten seats requires

  • Application to enrol at 15–20% → 55 to 70 applications needed
  • Landing page to application, cold search: 1.5–3%
  • Required cold clicks: 2,500 to 3,500

What search can actually deliver

  • Available impressions on the tight cluster, 25 days: 4,000–5,000
  • At 4–6% CTR on exact and phrase: 200 to 300 clicks
  • At ₹40–70 CPC: ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 of spend
The constraint on Google is inventory, not budget. Everything past roughly ₹25k is broad-match spill into franchise buyers, parents and job seekers. At a generous 3% conversion, 250 clicks returns 7 to 8 applications, so one or two seats. Worth running every cohort. Never the engine.

Also ruled out on Google

Performance Max needs conversion volume and its own 2 to 3 week learning period. Smart Bidding needs roughly 30 conversions per 30 days for target CPA to function. Until the account has a few cohorts of application history, this is a manual CPC account optimising on application starts, not on the sale.

Where six to eight of the ten actually come from

Paid is the assist, not the engine. The seats close on alumni referrals from six prior cohorts, the 40-person Mumbai mixer list from March, founder-led LinkedIn posting which is already performing, direct outreach to alternative-school operators, and one live info session, which is the highest-converting mechanic that exists for a high-ticket cohort product.

Clock 2

The always-on engine · from September

Built during the sprint, switched on the day Cohort 01 closes. Everything below is what turns a repeating scramble into a pipeline.

  • A waitlist, not a deadline. This is the single biggest unlock from running cohorts quarterly. Anyone who does not convert for September is not lost, they roll to November. A lead gets three or four cohort attempts, so the effective cost per seat falls with every cycle even if CPL stays flat.
  • Meta gets its creative testing budget from Cohort 02. By then the pixel has application history, the audience is seeded, and the learning compounds instead of expiring. This is the right time to do exactly what was originally proposed.
  • Always-on search at ₹15k to ₹25k a month. Never more, because the inventory is not there. Its job is brand capture and a steady one to two seats a cohort.
  • Nurture between cohorts. Every application that did not convert receives cohort content, alumni outcomes and Demo Day invitations until they take a seat or opt out.
06How to account for it

The sprint budget belongs to the engine, not to Cohort 01

Why the plan above is deliberately lean, and the one lever worth reviewing before Cohort 02.

At a minimum viable cohort size, acquisition cost has to be held deliberately low. That is why the sprint sits at ₹95,000 rather than the 15 to 20 percent of revenue a mature program would comfortably carry. Cohort 01 is being filled, not scaled.

Framed correctly, that ₹95,000 is not a Cohort 01 line item at all. Nearly all of it is capital cost on the engine: the landing page, the tracking, the audience seeding and the waitlist all persist. Amortised across the first four cohorts it lands at roughly ₹24,000 per cohort, or ₹2,400 per seat. That is the number to judge the spend on.

The lever worth reviewing before Cohort 02 is cohort ceiling. Moving the cap from ten to fifteen or twenty means the marginal seats carry the acquisition cost, and a nine-week hybrid format should absorb those without a proportional rise in delivery cost. That is the shift that makes the engine pay for itself from Cohort 03 onward.

07Before anyone spends

Three things to fix first

The first is a hard blocker. Nothing should go live until it is done.

1

The Google Form has to go

Applications currently run through a form linked in LinkedIn comments. You cannot run performance marketing into that: no conversion tracking, no message match, no ad-to-application continuity, no retargeting pixel. A real landing page with a tracked submit and a thank-you URL is deliverable one, and it is reusable for every cohort after this.

2

Price mechanics will move more than bidding will

₹1.5L as a single payment for a nine-week program is a genuine barrier in this market. A two-instalment or EMI option, plus an early-bird that visibly expires, will do more for seat count than any bid strategy in this plan.

3

Decide the day-12 fallback now

Fewer than 20 applications by 18/08/2026 means cutting paid, going all in on outreach and a second info session, and moving Cohort 01 to October. With a quarterly cadence that slip costs very little. Discounting the seat costs a great deal, because it permanently resets the price for every cohort after it.
