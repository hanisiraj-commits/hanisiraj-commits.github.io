Why Meta is not carrying August

Cold prospecting on Meta needs roughly 50 conversions per ad set per week to stabilise. This cohort offers ten. Add the two to three weeks it takes to find a winning creative and the window closes before there is anything to scale. Running it as a seat-filling channel this month would spend the budget and finish with nothing.

Run instead as retargeting and audience building, it needs none of that, because nothing is being asked to find anyone. It is cheap, it works from day one, and by Cohort 02 it will have the data behind it to do the prospecting properly.

02The demand picture

The category has no search demand

This is what drives the split above, so it is worth being exact about.

Query expansion on education fellowship india returns teacher fellowships, teaching fellowships and scholarships. Google does not recognise "a fellowship for people who want to build a school" as a concept that exists. There is no category-level demand to capture, at any budget.

What does exist is demand for the job

how to start a school in india carries a deep state-level tail across Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Telangana and Delhi. That depth is a reliable signal of real, sustained volume. Those searchers arrive looking for a licence or a franchise, so the landing page has to do the reframing, but they are reachable and they are worth reaching.

Consequence for the plan: search is a capture channel, not a volume channel. Sized correctly it is ₹20,000 and two to three seats. The volume has to come from targeting people by who they are, which is exactly what LinkedIn does and search cannot.

04Channel 1

LinkedIn Ads — ₹60,000

Owned by Tethrd. This is the channel expected to close the month.

The only place the exact person can be bought directly instead of being found by an algorithm. No learning phase to wait out, because the targeting is declared rather than inferred. Education is LinkedIn's cheapest vertical globally at roughly $4.18 CPC, and India sits well below platform average.

Audiences

  • A · Titles. Principal, Vice Principal, School Director, Head of School, Academic Director, Academic Head, Director of Curriculum, Founder, Co-Founder
  • B · Skills and groups. Curriculum Development, Educational Leadership, Instructional Design, School Management, plus India education group members
  • C · Matched. Alumni, mixer list and past applicants uploaded, with a lookalike layered once seeded
  • Geo. Mumbai, Bangalore, Delhi NCR, Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai

Setup

  • Formats. Single image, plus a Document ad carrying a real "what building a school actually takes" asset as the lead magnet
  • Conversation ads reserved for the matched list only
  • Objective. Native lead-gen form for cold, website conversion for warm
  • Bidding. Manual CPC, opened at the floor and raised only where applications appear
Expected: ₹1,500 to ₹3,000 per application, so 20 to 40 applications. This line alone should carry the cohort.
05Channel 2

Google Search — ₹20,000

Owned by Tethrd. Sized to the inventory, not to ambition.

Two campaigns

  • Brand. Uncapped, always on. Everyone who reads a LinkedIn post then googles the name lands here. Cheapest clicks in the account, but only ~90 searches a month, so it is insurance rather than a lead source.
  • Non-brand. Capped, running the exact and phrase list in section 03. This is the part doing the work.

Constraints

  • Manual CPC. Smart Bidding needs ~30 conversions per 30 days to function. Not available yet.
  • Performance Max off entirely. Needs volume and its own learning period.
  • Ceiling is real. The pool yields roughly 300 to 400 clicks across 25 days at 60 to 70% impression share. More budget does not buy more.
Expected: 10 to 15 applications. Brand will be the cheapest by a wide margin, non-brand will carry the volume.
06Channel 3

Meta — ₹20,000, in-house

Retargeting and audience building. Judged on the asset it produces, not on seats closed this month.

What runs now

  • Retargeting. Site visitors 180 days, Instagram engagers 365, Facebook engagers 365, video viewers at 50% 365, lead-form openers
  • Matched lists. Alumni, mixer attendees, past applicants uploaded as Custom Audiences
  • Objective. Leads. Never Purchase, there is nowhere near enough volume for it to optimise
  • Creative. Cuts from existing reels, founder to camera, Demo Day footage, alumni outcomes. Nothing new to produce
  • Frequency cap 3 per week. These pools are small and will fatigue fast

Lookalike audiences — the minimums

  • Hard minimum: 100 matched people from a single country. Below that Meta will not build a lookalike at all.
  • Practical minimum: 1,000 to 5,000. Under roughly 500 the signal is too thin and the lookalike behaves close to broad targeting.
  • CSV match rates in India run 40 to 70%. Upload every field available: email, phone with +91, first name, last name, city, country. More fields, higher match.
  • Start at 1% for closest match, widen to 3% only once volume proves out.

Seed the lookalike off intent, not off enrolments

Six cohorts of enrolled participants will not clear the practical threshold on their own. Stack every qualified signal into one source audience instead: all past applicants including those who did not enrol, mixer and Demo Day attendees, newsletter subscribers, Instagram and Facebook engagers at 365 days, video viewers past 50%, and website visitors once the pixel is live.

Combined, that should clear 1,000 comfortably. It is also a better seed than enrolments alone, because it describes the people who wanted this, which is the population being prospected into.

The highest-value Meta work this month is not the spend. It is getting the pixel live on day one and the lists uploaded in week one, so Cohort 02 can run cold prospecting properly instead of starting from zero again.
07Prerequisite

The landing page is deliverable one

Nothing goes live until this exists. It is not optional and it is not a nice-to-have.

You cannot advertise to a Google Form. No pixel can be installed on it, no conversion can be tracked back to a click, no visitor can be retargeted, and no ad message can be matched to what the page says. Every rupee spent pointing at one is spent blind.

The fix is straightforward. Ads point at a landing page. The landing page carries the link through to the existing Google Form. The form stays exactly as it is, so nothing about the application process has to change.

What the page has to do

  • Carry the Meta pixel, the Google tag and the LinkedIn insight tag
  • Fire a tracked event on the click through to the form
  • Reframe search arrivals who came looking for licensing and compliance
  • Hold the seat count, the September date and the nine-week format above the fold
  • Show alumni outcomes from the six cohorts already run

What it unlocks

  • Retargeting pools that build from day one
  • Cost per application, by channel and by audience
  • A lookalike seed that grows on its own
  • An asset that carries into Cohorts 02 and 03 unchanged
08Attribution

Can a Google Form be tracked back to the ads? No.

This decides whether any of the reporting above can be trusted, so it is worth being explicit about the mechanics.

Three hard blocks

  • No pixel or tag can be installed on a Google Form. Forms does not allow script injection, and the Analytics tracking field it once offered was removed.
  • The confirmation page cannot be tagged. "Your response has been recorded" is served from docs.google.com and is not yours to instrument.
  • There is no redirect on submit, so there is no thank-you URL for a conversion to fire on.

Net effect: Meta, LinkedIn and Google Ads have no way of knowing a submission happened. Spend pointed at a Google Form is spend measured by guesswork.

OptionWhat it gives youTrade-off
A · Move the form onto the landing page
Tally or Fillout		 A real submit event on all three platforms, plus the ability to retarget everyone who submitted. About an hour of setup. Both are free and both push responses into a Google Sheet, so nothing about the current workflow changes. Recommended
B · Keep the form, fire the conversion on the click through to it Each platform gets an event it can record and build audiences from. Measures application starts, not submits. Expect 40 to 70% completion on an application like this, so platform numbers will overstate.
C · Keep the form, pass the source into it
prefilled entry fields		 Add a short-answer field to the form, then have the landing page build the form link with source, campaign and click ID already filled in. Every response row then carries where it came from. That data lives in the Sheet, not in the ad platform. Accurate for reporting and for the day-12 budget call. Useless for platform optimisation.
Recommendation: Option A. If the Google Form has to stay exactly as it is, run B and C together. B gives the platforms something to fire on and build audiences from, C gives honest per-channel submit counts, and the two get reconciled at day 12.

Why this matters less than it looks, this month

Even with perfect submit-level tracking, none of these platforms will have the volume to optimise on it. Sixty applications across 25 days split three ways is roughly twenty per channel. Meta wants fifty per ad set per week. Google's Smart Bidding wants thirty per thirty days.

So the conversion event is not feeding an algorithm in August. It is doing two jobs: telling us where to move the ₹20,000 reserve at day 12, and building the retargeting and lookalike pools. Both are served adequately by B and C.

The reason to do Option A properly is Cohort 02, when the pixel has real history behind it and the optimisation actually starts to work.
