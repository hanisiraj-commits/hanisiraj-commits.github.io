03Learnings

What the data has taught us

From the 12-month repeat-purchase study (38,951 orders, 10,025 verified first-time buyers) plus this month's flow numbers.

The second order is a cross-sell, not a repeat

77.5% of second orders contain none of the products from the first. Customers don't rebuy their entry product — they explore. Yet Cross-Sell is currently the weakest earning flow (HK$3.4K in 90 days). The single biggest mismatch between what customers do and what the automation does.

Day 22–35 is the golden window

27.8% of new buyers reorder within 90 days, median 39 days — and the best gateway products reorder at day 22–35. Automation needs to be densest exactly there. Today that window is worked by one underweight Replenish flow (HK$14K/30d).

Staples drive repeat, heroes drive acquisition

Chicken, mince, salmon and pork-mince first orders repeat at 43–52%. Hero knife-deal boxes repeat at ~14%. Replenish and cross-sell content should push staples; the M9 heroes are for winning customers, not keeping them.

Checkout intent is worth 6× cart intent

Abandoned Checkout returns HK$75–78 per recipient vs HK$37–40 for cart — on a tenth of the volume (664 recipients/30d). Every extra person legitimately captured at checkout stage is the most valuable send in the account.

Scale is proven — the March rollout worked

The flow suite took recipients from ~5K to ~30K/month and share from 6% to 11–15%, with no damage to deliverability outside two watch items below. The playbook of building coverage stage-by-stage is validated; the remaining upside is in the stages still uncovered (chapter 05).

04Findings

Findings this month

Three things to fix, two things to watch.

Post-purchase nurture is barely triggering

BE-YOUR-OWN-BUTCHER post-purchase reached 8 people in 30 days against ~3,330 orders/month. When it does send, 75% open it. The audience clearly wants it — the trigger filters are throttling it. Highest-leverage fix in the account.

Cross-Sell earns least and unsubscribes most

HK$1.0K in 30 days with a 1.20% unsubscribe rate — the highest of any flow. Generic cross-sell content isn't matching what the transition data says people buy next. Rebuild it split by first-basket type (premium → staples, staples → wagyu trade-up).

Account hygiene: 45 flows, 13 earning

13 live flows had zero activity in 90 days (legacy seasonal, old tagging, duplicated alerts), 14 sit in draft, 5 in manual. None archived. Worth a one-hour sweep so reporting and QA stay clean.

Watch: share eased from the May peak

15.1% (May) → 12.5% (Jun) → 11.8% (Jul) while recipients held at ~30K — revenue per recipient softened, not volume. July's heavy promo calendar likely pulled orders forward of the flows. Watch August; if efficiency doesn't recover post-promo, we review message content and discount posture.

Watch: Welcome unsubscribe rate

0.91% on Welcome (vs ≤0.6% on everything else). Not urgent, but it's the first impression — worth revisiting frequency and email 2–4 content next optimisation pass.

03Learnings

What the first 100 days taught us

The UK account is young — these learnings decide which flows get built next, tested against the Hong Kong benchmark rather than cloned from it.

Flows punched in early

11.8% of order revenue within 90 days of launch, on essentially two working flows. Hong Kong's own history says each stage of coverage added compounds — the ceiling here is nowhere in sight.

Checkout recovery works in the UK too

£5.25 per recipient and an 11.9% click rate — the same efficiency pattern as Hong Kong's best flow. But it reached only 103 people in 30 days against 294 orders. Verify the trigger is catching every abandoned checkout before assuming that's true demand.

Launch-list quality shows in the unsubscribes

Welcome unsubscribes at 1.14% — roughly double a healthy rate. The Kickoff referral cohort converted well at launch but is colder than organic signups. Future intake should favour on-site capture over giveaway mechanics.

The reorder clock is already ticking

Hong Kong's benchmark: repeaters come back at median 39 days, best gateways at day 22–35. The June and July UK cohorts (~760 orders) are inside that window right now — and there is no live post-purchase, replenish or cross-sell flow to meet them.

04Findings

Findings this month

One structural risk, two mechanical fixes, one upstream constraint.

Everything after the first order is unbuilt

No live post-purchase, no cross-sell, no replenish at volume, and no winback flow exists in any state. With ~300 orders/month and a median 39-day reorder clock, this is where UK flow revenue is being left unearned. The post-purchase blueprint (03/08) is written and ready to build.

Replenish is live but reaching 2 people

2 recipients in 90 days means the trigger definition is too narrow, not that demand is absent. Quick diagnostic, quick fix.

List intake has dried up post-launch

Weekly account revenue fell from £8–13K in July to £2.9K in the first August week, and weekly flow recipients dropped from ~1,600 to ~240. Flows can only convert the traffic and signups they're fed — list growth is now the binding constraint on flow revenue.

Hygiene: three duplicate delivery-date alert drafts

Two SK-UK versions plus one HK clone of the same internal alert sit in drafts. Keep one, remove the rest.
