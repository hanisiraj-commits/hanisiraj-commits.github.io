The store was hot for reasons other than the deal

Fri–Sun ran +22% to +61% over benchmark with the deal at 1–7% of the day; Mon 10 was the one soft day (-27%), with the leg of lamb not selling a unit. M9+ Ribeye and Striploin are back in stock (6–8 a day each), conversion ran 1.9–2.3% against 1.6% BAU. Read that uplift as hero stock plus post-sale momentum.

Monday and Sunday are the deal doing its job

The striploin family (1kg, set, 2kg deal) did 95kg and $37.5k on Monday vs a Fri–Sun run rate of 32kg and $13.8k: 3x the kilos, about $23k added net of the 1kg listing dropping from ~18 to 6. The $348 Trio moved 66 items where those three SKUs average 12 a day loose, and 4 of its 14 buyers were new customers.

New-category and big-ticket tests (duck, leg of lamb, the $1,188 rib roast, hanger, USDA) landed at $0–5k. They rotate stock, but at 2–5 units they do not move the day.

The Trio is a different kind of win

Lowest price point run so far, 22 sets, and the highest share of first-time buyers of any deal (29%). Entry-price bundles look like the acquisition slot; the wagyu cuts are the revenue slot. Worth running one of each per week.

Early signs of a habit? A steady base, not yet a growing one

About 140–180 people a day arrive at the page directly, every day, including the three sale days when nothing new was on it. That is a real returning audience. But it is not compounding: direct sessions have sat between 96 and 197 a day for two weeks with no upward slope. The habit exists for a core; it is not spreading yet.

What would move it

Zero of the page's 4,700 sessions have come from email, and this week proved the offer sells without a send. The send should therefore point at tomorrow's deal and the daily-deal opt-in, and the page should show the week's rotation, so a visit has a reason to repeat.

The honest read

The deal is not training people to wait for discounts: deal buyers reorder at or slightly above the normal rate. But it is not acquiring either, and it is not yet creating a return visit. Six people out of 169 have bought two different deals. That is the number to move.

When the read gets solid

Only the July pilot has aged 28 days (9 of 29 reordered, 31%, but the Meat Emporium sat inside that window). The August cohort ages on 5 September; that is when the 28-day repeat rate becomes a real KPI.

06Next

Three things for the week ahead

  1. One revenue deal and one entry deal per week. Wagyu ribeye, wagyu striploin, rump cap and T-bone have each cleared $14k without a send at $776–$996; the $348 Trio brought new faces. The leg of lamb, the $1,188 rib roast and the $498 duck box are the "outside the proven cuts" data points. Today's M1-2 Ribeye 3kg at $998 sits $2 under the wall again; move it to $950 or $1,000, not in between.
  2. Turn the daily traffic into daily buyers. 346 people a day are already landing on the page and 47% of them came direct. Show the week's rotation on the page, and use email for tomorrow's deal rather than today's, so the visit repeats.
  3. Carry the scoreboard weekly. Deals-page direct sessions, second-deal buyers, and 7/28-day repeat vs benchmark are now the three programme KPIs alongside deal revenue. Beat the Clock (Hextom pending with David) is the first mechanic aimed at the return visit; run it on a proven cut so the read is clean.
