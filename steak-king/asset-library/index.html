1 · Steak King Henry — the character

Henry is Steak King's mascot cow. He started as a physical brand mascot and we have been generating him as a hero character across campaigns. He is the closest thing the account has to a recurring brand device, and he is the piece that most needs a written usage rule.

The one rule that governs every Henry image: the steaks get the realism, Henry never does. Cartoon character, real meat. Photoreal Henry has been rejected outright — with real fur he stops reading as a mascot and becomes an animal. Stylised steaks have also been rejected — the meat is the product and has to look like food.

Canonical design — the details that keep getting lost

  • Spiky orange crown, not a cap. This is the single most common drift.
  • Curved gold horns emerging beneath the crown.
  • Matte white plush body — soft vinyl finish, never glossy, never furry.
  • Irregular black splat spots on body and limbs. No udder.
  • Red quilted sleeveless gilet with gold roundel emblems and Chinese frog buttons.
  • "STEAK KING HENRY" stacked on three lines across the chest, heavy black sans.
  • Gold cowbell on a red collar. Chunky black mitten hooves and boots.
  • Warm gold rim light, bipedal chibi proportions, deep black ground.

What has been rejected, and why

Worth a look before you start — these are the four failure modes we have already paid for.

Base generation prompt

This is the prompt we have converged on. It works with the master reference image attached — the reference does most of the work, the prompt holds the details the model keeps dropping.

Steak King Henry, a chibi bipedal cartoon cow mascot, full body, LANDSCAPE 16:9.

BODY: matte white plush finish, soft vinyl toy surface, never glossy and never furry.
Rounded chibi proportions, oversized head, short limbs. Irregular black splat spots
on the body and limbs. No udder. Large glossy black oval eyes with three thick black
eyelashes above each eye. Big soft cream muzzle with two large black nostrils. Small
pink inner ears.

HEAD: a spiky ORANGE CROWN (a crown, not a cap) sits on top of his head, with curved
GOLD horns emerging beneath it.

COSTUME: red quilted sleeveless gilet with gold circular roundel emblems and Chinese
frog-button closures, worn open over the white body. "STEAK KING HENRY" printed in
heavy black sans-serif across his chest, stacked on three lines. Gold cowbell on a
red collar. Chunky black mitten hooves and black boots.

LIGHT: warm gold rim light, deep black background.

MEAT: any steaks in frame are PHOTOREAL — real marbling, real texture, real food
photography. The steaks carry the realism, Henry never does.

Negative prompt

Photoreal or furry Henry. Real animal fur or real cow anatomy. Glossy plastic
sheen. A baseball cap or backwards cap instead of the crown. An udder. Stylised or
illustrated steaks. Portrait crop. Wordmark on one or two lines instead of three.

Process rules we learned the hard way

  • Crop the character out before you generate. Three rounds of drift ended the moment Henry was cropped away from the email layout. Feed a full email as reference and the model infers the character from the layout instead of the character.
  • Never feed a previous render back in as a reference. It flips the crop to portrait and pushes the style toward vector. Always go back to the master reference.
  • State "LANDSCAPE 16:9" explicitly every time.
  • There is no fine dial on realism. Asking for "slightly less real" twice returns the same image. The steps are baseline, one notch, then straight to heavy stylisation.
  • The model cannot do a wind-blasted face. Pushed three ways, it always reverts to a normal grin. That one is a hand-execution job.
  • The costume swaps per campaign, the silhouette never does. Uncle Sam for the USDA sale, default red gilet and orange crown for HK. Crown, horns, bell, splat spots and the three-line chest wordmark survive every swap.
